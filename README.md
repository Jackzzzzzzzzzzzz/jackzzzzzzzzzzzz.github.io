## About

I study how gene regulation shapes intestinal epithelial function and mucosal immunity. My work bridges molecular and cellular biology, bioinformatics, and machine learning. More broadly, I am interested in using multi-omics approaches to understand the molecular mechanisms underlying cancer and chronic inflammatory disease.

## Current Research

My doctoral research focuses on hnRNP I (PTBP1), an RNA-binding protein involved in RNA processing. Using a mouse model of epithelial hnRNP I loss, I examine changes in antimicrobial defense, epithelial specialization, and mucosal immune organization through transcriptomic analyses, molecular assays, and quantitative imaging.

## Research Snapshot

### Spatial mapping of intestinal epithelial organization

I combine tissue imaging, single-cell reference mapping, and spatial transcriptomics to study epithelial organization in the colon. Computational unrolling maps annotated tissue sections onto a shared anatomical coordinate system, allowing cell states and gene-expression programs to be compared along the colon and across the depth of the epithelium.
